Case File 01

The Cafeteria Files

A psychology student sits quietly at a corner table every day for two weeks, clipboard in hand. She never talks to anyone or asks a single question. She just writes down who sits where, how loud each table gets, and how long groups stay after the bell rings. She never lets the students know she's watching.
Task 1 — Name the Method

Which research method is this? Write your group's answer in your ISN before revealing.

Task 2 — The Gotcha

A teammate says this is a survey because she's "collecting data on people." What's actually missing that would make it a real survey?

Task 3 — Trust Rating

Rate this case 1–5 on how well it can prove cause and effect. Defend it in one sentence.

Findings Confirmed

Method: Naturalistic observation — watching behavior in a real, natural setting without interfering.

Gotcha answer: A survey requires directly asking participants questions. She never asked anyone anything — she only watched and recorded. No questions asked means no survey.

Trust rating guide: Around 2–3/5 for cause and effect. It captures real behavior, but it can't explain why students act that way, and results from one cafeteria may not generalize elsewhere.

Case File 02

The Lunch Line Poll

A teacher hands out a five-question form asking students to rate how stressed they feel on a scale of 1–10, how many hours they sleep, and whether they check their phone before bed. Two hundred students across three grade levels fill it out anonymously in one class period.
Task 1 — Name the Method

Which research method is this? Write your group's answer in your ISN before revealing.

Task 2 — The Gotcha

A teammate says this can't count because "no one is being interviewed in person." Is that correct?

Task 3 — Trust Rating

Rate this case 1–5 on how well it can prove cause and effect. Defend it in one sentence.

Findings Confirmed

Method: Survey — a list of questions given to a sample of people, gathering self-reported data.

Gotcha answer: Incorrect. Surveys don't have to be spoken interviews — a written questionnaire still counts as a survey. What makes it a survey is that participants are directly asked questions, whether on paper, online, or aloud.

Trust rating guide: Around 2–3/5 for cause and effect, but strong for spotting patterns across a large group. The weak point is self-reported data — students might misremember or answer in ways that make them look good.

Case File 03

The Viral Meltdown

After a popular teen influencer had a public breakdown live on stream, a psychologist spent six months interviewing her, her family, and her former manager, while also reviewing years of her old posts, to understand exactly what led to that moment.
Task 1 — Name the Method

Which research method is this? Write your group's answer in your ISN before revealing.

Task 2 — The Gotcha

A teammate says this is archival research because they "reviewed old posts." What's the key difference?

Task 3 — Trust Rating

Rate this case 1–5 on how well it can prove cause and effect. Defend it in one sentence.

Findings Confirmed

Method: Case (clinical) study — an in-depth look at one individual using multiple sources of information.

Gotcha answer: Archival research only uses existing records, with no new contact with people. This case involved brand-new interviews with the influencer and people around her — the old posts were just one piece of a much deeper, ongoing investigation into one person.

Trust rating guide: Around 1–2/5 for cause and effect and for generalizing to other people. Case studies give incredible depth on one person but the findings may not apply to teens or influencers in general.

Case File 04

The Screen Time Study

Researchers gave 150 students an app that automatically logged their daily screen time, then had each student separately rate their mood every night for a month. When the two sets of numbers were graphed against each other, students with more screen time tended to report lower mood — but not always.
Task 1 — Name the Method

Which research method is this? Write your group's answer in your ISN before revealing.

Task 2 — The Gotcha

A teammate insists this proves phones cause bad moods. What's the counter-argument?

Task 3 — Trust Rating

Rate this case 1–5 on how well it can prove cause and effect. Defend it in one sentence.

Findings Confirmed

Method: Correlational study — measuring two existing variables to see if they're related, without manipulating anything.

Gotcha answer: Correlation is not causation. A confounding variable — like poor sleep or stress — could be driving both higher screen time and lower mood at the same time, without either one directly causing the other.

Trust rating guide: Around 1–2/5 for proving cause and effect, but 4/5 for spotting a real relationship worth investigating further with an actual experiment.

Case File 05

The Yearbook Investigation

A class pulled forty years of the school's yearbooks out of the library archive and coded every club photo by number of members, comparing decades to see if extracurricular participation has changed over time. No current students were interviewed.
Task 1 — Name the Method

Which research method is this? Write your group's answer in your ISN before revealing.

Task 2 — The Gotcha

A teammate says this is a longitudinal study because it spans decades. What's the actual difference?

Task 3 — Trust Rating

Rate this case 1–5 on how well it can prove cause and effect. Defend it in one sentence.

Findings Confirmed

Method: Archival research — studying existing records to look for patterns, without collecting any new data from people.

Gotcha answer: A longitudinal study follows the same people forward in time, collecting new data from them again and again. This investigation never contacted a single person — it only reused records that already existed, which is what makes it archival.

Trust rating guide: Around 2–3/5. It reveals real historical trends, but researchers have no control over what was recorded or how — some clubs may never have been photographed at all.

Case File 06

The Elevator Test

Researchers randomly assigned volunteers into two groups. Group A rode a rigged elevator where a stranger stood facing backward, toward the rear wall, the entire ride. Group B rode the same elevator, but the stranger faced forward like normal. Researchers then measured how many seconds it took each volunteer to start subtly mimicking the stranger's body position.
Task 1 — Name the Method

Which research method is this? Write your group's answer in your ISN before revealing.

Task 2 — The Gotcha

A teammate says Case 01 (the cafeteria) was also an experiment because researchers were "testing" something. Why is this one actually different?

Task 3 — Trust Rating

Rate this case 1–5 on how well it can prove cause and effect. Defend it in one sentence.

Findings Confirmed

Method: Experiment — random assignment plus a manipulated independent variable (the stranger's facing direction) and a measured dependent variable (time to mimic).

Gotcha answer: Case 01 only observed behavior that was already happening — nothing was changed or controlled. This case actually manipulates a variable and randomly assigns people to conditions, which is the only way to make a real cause-and-effect claim.

Trust rating guide: 4–5/5 for cause and effect — this is exactly what true experiments are built for. The trade-off is that a staged elevator is more artificial than a real-world setting.
